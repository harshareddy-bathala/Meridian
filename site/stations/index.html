Join with a station

Have an antenna? Join the network.

A ground station that joins Meridian gets told which passes are worth its time, gets warned when it starts to fail, and gets a plain report of what it caught. It never has to run our software to do it.

Invite only, for now Station 001, the team's own, is being built. Other stations join by invitation while the network is small.

What you need.

  • A small computer

    A Raspberry Pi 4 or 5 is plenty. Any Linux machine that can stay on works too.

  • A radio receiver

    A USB software-defined radio, the kind sold for receiving weather satellites and aircraft.

  • An antenna for 137 MHz

    A V-dipole made from two rods works; a QFH, which you can build at home, works better.

  • Sky and internet

    A view of as much sky as you can manage, and a connection to report back over.

What you get back.

  • A schedule

    The passes most likely to work from your roof, planned with every other station so none is wasted.

  • Early warnings

    A health warning when your station starts to slip, and a likely cause for every pass it loses.

  • Plain reports

    What was planned, what worked and why the rest did not, with nothing counted against you for the hours your station was switched off.

It only ever listens.

A Meridian station receives and never transmits. There is no transmit code anywhere in the project, and there will not be. Receiving needs no licence almost everywhere; transmitting does, and a network that quietly enabled it would hand its members a legal problem.

One open protocol.

Stations talk to Meridian through MSP, a short, published protocol: register, send a heartbeat, collect the schedule, report what was received. Anything that follows it can join, whatever software it runs. It is small enough to implement on a microcontroller.

If you would rather not write your own, the reference station client runs on a Raspberry Pi, and installs without any database or web server.

Ground station

A receiver on the ground (an antenna, a radio and a small computer) that listens to satellites as they fly over.

Every term

Pass

The few minutes a satellite spends above a station's horizon. Each pass happens once. If it is missed, its data is gone.

Every term

Health warning

An early notice that a station is slowly getting worse, sent to its owner before it fails outright.

Every term

Loss cause

When a pass returns nothing useful, Meridian's best explanation: view blocked, radio interference, clock wrong, receiver off or satellite silent. When the evidence does not point anywhere, it says 'undetermined' instead of guessing.

Every term

MSP

Meridian Station Protocol: the open, published rules a station follows to join the network. Any station that follows them can take part, whatever software it runs.

Every term

Heartbeat

A short message a station sends regularly to say what it is doing. It is how Meridian tells a station that was listening apart from one that was switched off.

Every term

Meridian

Open software that decides which satellite passes are worth catching, catches them, checks the result and says what happened.
