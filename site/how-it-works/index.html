How it works

What happens to one satellite pass.

A pass lasts a few minutes and never comes back. Here is what Meridian does with one, from choosing it to telling the station's owner how it went.

Practice fleet Today these steps run on 50 practice stations Simulated. The first real station is being built.

  1. 1 · Predict

    Pick the passes worth catching.

    Not every pass is worth a station's time. Meridian scores each one by how likely it is to return data, its predicted yield, from how high it climbs, how fresh its orbit numbers are and how this station has done before.

  2. 2 · Choose

    Plan every station at once.

    With more passes than time, Meridian draws up one schedule for the whole network: the best passes covered, no station booked twice, nothing caught twice for no reason. The obvious plan, highest pass first, is kept beside it as the yardstick.

  3. 3 · Receive

    Listen, and prove it.

    While it records, the station sends a heartbeat saying what it is doing. That is how Meridian tells a station that heard nothing from one that was switched off, and only the first is a miss.

  4. 4 · Check

    Say how sure we are the data is usable.

    The recording is decoded, for the Meteor-M satellites' LRPT signal into an image, and given a reception confidence: how likely it is to be usable. When it says 80%, about eight in ten really are.

  5. 5 · Explain

    When a pass fails, say why.

    A lost pass gets a cause from the evidence: the station's sky, its recent passes, its heartbeats and its clock. Without enough evidence, the answer is "undetermined", never a guess.

  6. 6 · Warn

    Catch a station getting worse.

    Antennas loosen and trees grow. Meridian learns each station's normal and sends a health warning when it starts to slip, before it fails.

  7. 7 · Tell and share

    Tell the owner, and publish the evidence.

    The owner gets a plain report of what was planned, caught and lost, and why. Everything behind it goes into an evidence dataset anyone can download and check.

Pass

The few minutes a satellite spends above a station's horizon. Each pass happens once. If it is missed, its data is gone.

All terms

Predicted yield

Meridian's estimate of how likely a pass is to return usable data. It answers 'will this work?', not just 'will the satellite be visible?'.

All terms

Elevation

How high a satellite climbs above the horizon during a pass, in degrees, from 0 at the horizon to 90 straight overhead. Higher usually means a cleaner signal, but it is one clue among several.

All terms

Element set

The published numbers that describe a satellite's orbit, used to predict where it will be. They age: a week-old set predicts less accurately than yesterday's.

All terms

Schedule

Which station listens to which pass, decided for all stations at once, so the most promising passes are covered and no station is asked to be in two places.

All terms

Heartbeat

A short message a station sends regularly to say what it is doing. It is how Meridian tells a station that was listening apart from one that was switched off.

All terms

Decode

Turning a raw radio recording into the satellite's actual data. For a weather satellite, that means an image.

All terms

LRPT

The digital picture signal sent by the Meteor-M weather satellites on 137 MHz. It is what Meridian's first station is built to receive.

All terms

Reception confidence

How sure Meridian is that a reception's data is usable, as a percentage. It is checked against what really happened: of the passes marked 80%, about 80 in 100 should turn out good.

All terms

Loss cause

When a pass returns nothing useful, Meridian's best explanation: view blocked, radio interference, clock wrong, receiver off or satellite silent. When the evidence does not point anywhere, it says 'undetermined' instead of guessing.

All terms

Health warning

An early notice that a station is slowly getting worse, sent to its owner before it fails outright.

All terms

Evidence dataset

A sealed, downloadable copy of every pass and its result, with the settings used, so anyone can check Meridian's figures for themselves.

All terms

Meridian

Open software that decides which satellite passes are worth catching, catches them, checks the result and says what happened.
